                    Quarterly information report as of the end of September 2026


                    Robust growth in a challenging macroeconomic context
              (+6% at constant exchange rates and +3% at current exchange rates)


                                                                                                      Paris, October 22, 2026



The group’s consolidated revenue at the end of September 2026 amounted to €12.3 billion, up
6% at constant exchange rates and 3% at current exchange rates compared to the same period
in 2025. The Americas, Japan and Europe excluding France achieved particularly dynamic
growth.

Third quarter sales reached €4.1 billion, in line with second-quarter sales, up 4% at constant
exchange rates and 6% at current exchange rates. All regions recorded growth, with the
exception of the Middle East, despite the deteriorating macroeconomic and geopolitical
context.

Axel Dumas, Executive Chairman of Hermès, commented:
“Despite a complex environment, Hermès continues its profitable growth and maintains its
course. Convinced of the strength and relevance of our artisanal model, we see our vision as
firmly rooted in the long term, and we look to the future with confidence. It is with great
enthusiasm that we prepare for a new year rich in creation, with the first men’s ready-to-wear
collection signed by Grace Wales Bonner and the introduction of Couture by Nadège Vanhée
next January.”


Sales by geographical area at the end of September
(at constant exchange rates unless otherwise indicated)

At the end of September 2026, all the geographical areas posted growth with the exception of
the Middle East, slightly down.

• Asia excluding Japan (+2%) continued its growth, supported in particular by a solid
  performance in Korea. Greater China was stable over the period in a more difficult context.
  In April, the new Sanlitun store opened in Beijing, followed by the renovated and expanded
  stores of Hong Kong Elements and Sogo Fuxing in Taipei. Finally, the Kaohsiung store in
  Taiwan and the Taikoo Li mall store in Chengdu reopened following renovation and
  expansion work in August and September respectively.

• Japan (+11%) confirmed solid and steady momentum, driven by the loyalty of its local
  customer base. The new store at Hilton Plaza East in Osaka was inaugurated in May,
  following renovation and expansion work, before the opening of the new Nagoya location
  in June.

•   The Americas (+13%) achieved strong growth across all countries and métiers. The San Diego store,
    renovated and expanded, reopened its doors in July, followed by the openings of new stores at Plaza del
    Lago in Chicago in August and Williamsburg in New York in September. In Brazil, the Barra da Tijuca
    store in Rio de Janeiro also opened its doors in August. An event dedicated to petit h creations was
    presented at the Ala Moana store in Honolulu, Hawaii, in August.

•   Europe excluding France (+9%) maintained sustained growth, thanks in particular to the dynamism of
    Southern Europe. The United Kingdom also recorded a strong performance in the third quarter, driven
    by the opening a new Maison at 166 New Bond Street in London, successfully inaugurated on June 16.

•   France (+2%) progressed, supported by the solid performance of seaside stores and the loyalty of the
    local customer base.

•   The Other area (-4%), which mainly includes the Middle East, continued to show solid resilience in a
    geopolitical context that remains complex.


Sales by sector at the end of September
(at constant exchange rates unless otherwise indicated)

At the end of September 2026, the Leather Goods and Saddlery métier and the Silk and Textiles métier
showed solid growth.

•   Leather Goods and Saddlery (+10%) demonstrated remarkable performance with a slight acceleration in
    the third quarter, driven by strong desirability of both new models and iconic pieces. Collections have
    been enriched, notably with the Double Longe, Besace Attelée and Bride de Jour models. The increase
    in production capacity continued with the inauguration in early April of a twenty-fifth leather goods
    workshop in Loupes (Gironde). Three new leather goods workshops will open in the coming years:
    Charleville-Mézières (Ardennes) by 2027, Colombelles (Calvados) by 2028 and Les Andelys (Eure) by
    2030. Hermès thus continues to reinforce its presence in France by developing employment and training
    in eleven regions.

•   The Ready-to-wear and Accessories sector (+1%) experienced slight growth. The second chapter of the
    women’s fall-winter 2026 collection was successfully presented in June in Los Angeles, followed by the
    spring-summer 2027 collection, presented in early October at the Garde Républicaine in Paris, which
    also received a warm welcome.

•   The Silk and Textiles sector (+7%) achieved solid performance thanks to the richness of its creations, in
    both women’s and men’s collections. The Chevaux d’Héphaïstos and La Nature en Voyage scarves
    reinterpreted the grammar of the carré with modernity.

•   Perfume and Beauty (-1%) recorded a slight decline, with an improvement in the third quarter. The
    Perfume collections welcomed a new Eau de parfum with a solar signature, Barénia Pleine Fleur, as
    well as Ginseng Biloba, a woody and radiant Hermessence. Hermès Beauty introduced a lip care ritual
    with a mask and balm, which met with immediate success.

•   The Watches métier (-2%) continued the enrichment of its offering, notably through the successful
    launch of the H08 squelette model, equipped with the latest generation titanium movement, unveiled last
    April at the Watches and Wonders trade show in Geneva. The strengthening of production capacities
    continues, with the expansion of the watchmaking site in Le Noirmont in Switzerland by 2028.

•   The Other Hermès sectors (+3%), which include Jewellery and the Home Universe, recorded growth,
    illustrating the singularity and creative strength of the house. The ninth Haute Bijouterie collection, Into
    the Horsescape, was successfully unveiled in early July in Paris, at the Musée des Arts Décoratifs, before
    making a stop in Shanghai in September.

A sustainable and responsible model

True to its sustainable artisanal model, Hermès integrates the management of its environmental impact into
a long-term approach.

As part of its commitment to nature, the house continued to progress within the Science Based Targets for
Nature (SBTN) initiative. Following the validation of the first two steps, Hermès has launched two pilot
projects focusing on soil and freshwater, as part of the third step relating to the target-setting.

This sustainable and responsible approach was recognized in August by the rating agency MSCI, which
confirmed its AA rating, in a context of increased requirements and transparency, a testament to the house’s
commitments and values.


Other highlights

At the end of September 2026, currency fluctuations represented a negative impact of approximately €310
million on revenue.

During the first nine months, Hermès International redeemed 94 846 shares for €160 million, excluding
transactions completed within the framework of the liquidity contract.


Outlook

In the medium-term, despite the economic, geopolitical and monetary uncertainties around the world, the
group confirms an ambitious goal for revenue growth at constant exchange rates.

The group continues its development with confidence, thanks to the highly integrated artisanal model,
balanced distribution network, the creativity of collections and the loyalty of clients.

Thanks to its unique business model, Hermès is pursuing its long-term development strategy based on
creativity, maintaining control over know-how and singular communication.

The theme for the year, Venture beyond, is an invitation to discover new horizons and renew our curiosity,
constantly.
